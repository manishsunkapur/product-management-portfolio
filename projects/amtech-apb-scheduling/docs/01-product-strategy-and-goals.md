# 01 · Product Strategy and Goals

## Why this phase comes first

Begin with the company and product context: who the product serves, the value it is meant to create, and the business outcome this work should support. This keeps discovery focused without assuming that the first feature idea is the right opportunity.

Public Amtech materials position APB within packaging scheduling and describe downtime response. The internal product vision, current roadmap, customer priorities, and quarterly goals are not available in this exercise; those would be confirmed with Amtech’s Product Manager and leadership.

## Strategic fit hypothesis

**Product area:** Automated production scheduling for corrugated packaging operations.

**Customer value hypothesis:** Help plants recover from disruptions while maintaining feasible schedules, delivery commitments, and operational control.

**Candidate opportunity:** Make the planner’s decision after downtime easier to understand and execute, if discovery confirms that this is a repeated and costly pain.

This is a fit hypothesis for the work sample, not a statement of Amtech’s approved strategy or roadmap.

## Draft objective

**Objective:** Help production schedulers recover from downtime faster while maintaining a feasible, trusted, and stable lineup.

This objective will be revised after customer discovery and alignment with Amtech’s actual quarterly goals. The numeric key results belong in [Phase 5: PRD, OKRs, and success metrics](05-prd-okrs-and-success-metrics.md), after the problem and pilot baseline are understood.

## Strategy questions for the interview

- Which customer segment and product line is the role accountable for: corrugated, labels, or both?
- What customer and company outcome is the scheduling roadmap expected to improve this quarter?
- How does the existing roadmap address schedule stability, production visibility, delivery performance, and adoption?
- What product principles matter most for this workflow: reliability, explainability, configurable plant rules, or faster automation?
- What is the evidence threshold for introducing or changing scheduling behavior in a live plant?

## Decision guardrails

1. **Customer problem before feature:** Validate a repeated user need and its operational impact before treating the proposed concept as a gap.
2. **Fit before scope:** Confirm the opportunity supports the actual product strategy and target segment.
3. **Trust before automation:** Preserve clear review and recovery paths until customers and the product team establish safe automation rules.
4. **Outcomes before output:** Measure recovery time, feasibility, delivery, and stability—not screens shipped or proposals generated alone.
5. **Evidence before targets:** Set numerical KRs after collecting a baseline and agreeing on definitions with pilot customers.

## Framework use

- Use **OKRs** to connect the quarterly objective to measurable customer outcomes.
- Use **SMART** as a quality check for a key result: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound.
- Treat company-level vision and quarterly strategy as context; feature-level OKRs are hypotheses until discovery and baseline data support them.
